  • A physics-based computational forward model for efficient image reconstruction in magnetic particle imaging
    Toby Sanders, Justin Konkle, Olivia C Sehl, A Rahman Mohtasebzadeh, Joan M Greve, Patrick W Goodwill — IEEE TMI (2025) — Link
    In this research a physics-based way to reconstruct MPI images is develed that doesn’t rely on calibration scans, works across different scan setups, and scales to high resolution. The core MPI signal equations were turned into a small sequence of fast steps that account for how the scanner moves, how strong the fields are, how sensitive the coils are, and how the signal is filtered. The algorithm was tested on simulated and preclinical data, this method produces accurate images efficiently.
  • Preclinical and Clinical-Scale Magnetic Particle Imaging of Natural Killer Cells: in vitro and ex vivo Demonstration of Cellular Sensitivity, Resolution, and Quantification
    Olivia C Sehl, Yanwen Yang, Ariana R Anjier, Dmitry Nevozhay, Donghang Cheng, Kelvin Guo, Benjamin Fellows, Abdul Rahman Mohtasebzadeh, Erica E Mason, Toby Sanders, Petrina Kim, David Trease, Dimpy Koul, Patrick W Goodwill, Konstantin Sokolov, Max Wintermark, Nancy Gordon, Joan M Greve, Vidya Gopalakrishnan — Molecular Imaging and Biology (2025) — Link
    In this collaborative resaerch with MD Anderson - Houston, Western University, we labeled NK-92 immune cells with iron-oxide nanoparticles and confirmed their killing ability and viability were unchanged. Using Magnetic Particle Imaging (MPI), we tracked these cells quantitatively and accurately in mouse brain, bone, and lung, detecting as few as ~3.1×10⁴ cells and localizing them to within <1 mm of their injection sites; we also showed feasibility on a clinical-scale scanner at a dose (4×10⁷ cells) similar to prior trials. Together, we show MPI can provide early, sensitive readouts of NK cell delivery and location—addressing a key need for monitoring cell therapy in patients.
  • Magnetic particle imaging enables nonradioactive quantitative sentinel lymph node identification: feasibility proof in murine models
    Olivia C Sehl, Kelvin Guo, Abdul Rahman Mohtasebzadeh, Petrina Kim, Benjamin Fellows, Marcela Weyhmiller, Patrick W Goodwill, Max Wintermark, Stephen Y Lai, Paula J Foster, Joan M Greve — Radiology Advances (2024) — Link
    We tested a nonradioactive, magnetic alternative to sentinel lymph node biopsy using Magnetic Particle Imaging (MPI) lymph node mapping. We injected iron-oxide tracers in mice and used MPI to sensitively and quantitatively map the first draining lymph node across several body regions within 1 hour, with clear signal lasting 4–6 days and matching ex vivo validation. These results show MPI can reliably identify sentinel nodes long enough to plan surgery, supporting future clinical translation as clinical-scale MPI matures.
  • Tunability and Ordering in 2D Arrays of Magnetic Nanoparticles Assembled via Extreme Field Gradients.
    A. R. Mohtasebzadeh, J. C. Davidson, K. L. Livesey, T. M. Crawford — Advanced Materials Interfaces (2022) — Link
    We directed magnetite nanoparticles to self-assemble on hard-disk media using nanoscale magnetic field gradients. When two magnetic reversals were closely spaced, the particles formed a single, centered feature with hexatic ordering, which our Langevin simulations traced to the medium’s magnetic properties. Small changes in solvent polarity disrupted the ordering and nudged particles toward the transitions, providing a simple handle to fine-tune placement.
  • Magnetic‐Field‐Directed Self‐Assembly of Programmable Mesoscale Shapes.
    Longfei Ye, Tanner Pearson, Cory Dolbashian, Philip Pstrak, A. R. Mohtasebzadeh, Ben Fellows, O Thompson Mefford, Thomas M Crawford — Advanced Functional Materials (2016) — Link
    We used perpendicular magnetic recording (PMR) media to “write” templates that direct magnetic nanoparticles to self-assemble into user-designed 2D shapes, with features as small as a single 30 nm particle. The particles line up at boundaries between oppositely magnetized regions—where the magnetic gradients are strongest—forming 1–2 layers with widths from ~30 to 350 nm. Because lateral forces extend along the surface, features grow sideways before stacking, pointing to a simple way to control layer-by-layer assembly through template design.
  • Magnetic nanoparticle arrays self-assembled on perpendicular magnetic recording media.
    A.R. Mohtasebzadeh, Longfei Ye, Thomas M Crawford— International Journal of Molecular Sciences (2015) — Link
    We directed magnetic nanoparticles to self-assemble on templates written into perpendicular recording media and tracked how the features grow over time. Early assemblies were narrow and one-particle tall (~150 nm wide, ~14 nm high), while longer times produced wider, taller structures (~550 nm wide, ~35 nm high) with higher aspect ratios and stronger collective magnetic behavior. These trends point to intense field gradients near recorded transitions driving growth, suggesting a clear path to tune assembly and build more complex, heterogeneous materials.
